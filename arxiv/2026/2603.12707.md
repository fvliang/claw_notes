## 摘要（英文）
Multimodal large language model (MLLM) inference splits into two phases with opposing hardware demands: vision encoding is compute-bound, while language generation is memory-bandwidth-bound. We show that under standard transformer KV caching, the modality boundary minimizes cross-device transfer among all partition points. Partitioning here reduces transfer complexity from O(L*s_ctx) bytes (GB-scale KV caches under stage-level disaggregation) to O(N_v*d) bytes (MB-scale embeddings), an O(L) reduction. The result holds across attention mechanisms (MHA/GQA), dynamic vision resolutions, and model scales. A direct implication is that modality-level disaggregation enables cross-tier heterogeneous serving over commodity PCIe. A closed-form cost model shows that heterogeneous deployment is cost-optimal under phase-separable workloads (predicts 31.4% savings; observed 40.6%). We build HeteroServe, a phase-aware runtime with modality-level partitioning and cross-tier scheduling. On identical 4xA100 hardware, engine optimizations raise throughput by up to 54%. Under a fixed budget, a heterogeneous cluster ($38k) improves Tokens/$ by 37% over a homogeneous baseline ($64k) without degrading latency.
